Behind the launch of an AI enterprise plan is a vendor's need to stabilize revenue with long-term corporate contracts once individual subscriptions reach scale. In an interview uploaded in late January 2026 on the Chinese podcast Kedaibiao Lizheng, Hockey Stick founder Chen Chang and the host broke down this pattern: when product-led growth companies reach tens of millions of dollars in annual revenue, high churn among individual prosumers and small businesses makes growth hard to sustain, whereas moving upmarket to enterprise customers brings in 1-year or 3-to-5-year contracts. Enterprise buyers who understand why vendors shift SaaS pricing can negotiate clearer terms on seats, usage, and permissions.

## Why companies add enterprise sales at tens of millions in revenue

In the Kedaibiao Lizheng interview, Chen Chang said that product-led growth companies reaching roughly tens of millions of dollars in annual revenue almost always start building an enterprise motion.

She explained that self-serve growth can be explosive and converts users well, yet when a company sells mainly to individual prosumers and small businesses, churn stays high; by the time revenue reaches tens of millions of dollars, most companies feel churn pressure because the absolute dollar amount churned grows larger every month.

Chen Chang named several examples in the interview: Notion, Slack, Gamma, which she noted recently introduced enterprise pricing, her former company Otter, and HeyGen all started building enterprise sales around that stage. The host of Kedaibiao Lizheng said he had noticed this shift before and assumed companies had hit a market ceiling, rather than realizing it was driven by churn; from a growth accounting standpoint it makes sense, since higher absolute churn makes the growth curve harder to maintain.

## How annual contracts differ from monthly subscriptions

Chen Chang compared the two revenue streams in the interview: enterprise revenue is much higher quality, locked in for at least a year and often three to five years, compared with individuals buying monthly plans; on the other hand, enterprise-focused companies grow more slowly and lack overnight viral spikes. The host added that closing each enterprise deal takes exhausting work.

From an enterprise buyer's perspective, a vendor launching an AI enterprise plan is looking for steadier revenue. When a company requests a quote, the vendor will usually steer the deal toward an annual contract, leaving less room to cancel month to month.

## Base subscription plus credits as the main billing structure

Alongside the shift from monthly plans to annual contracts, billing structures are changing too. Chen Chang noted in the interview that subscription billing remains the mainstream model, while many credit-based companies now pair a small base subscription with extra credit purchases when usage rises.

This SaaS pricing structure of a base subscription plus top-up credits, combined with switching from monthly payments to annual contracts, requires companies to update both budget planning and renewal tracking. Where multiplying headcount by a fixed monthly fee once locked in full-year spend, buyers now have to factor in extra credits consumed during peak business periods; for keeping annual budgets under control with usage billing, see <NoteLink slug="ai-usage-pricing-budget">Can an Annual IT Budget Handle Usage-Based AI Pricing?</NoteLink>.

## Clauses to compare before signing an enterprise purchase

Before purchasing, compare the individual subscription plan and the enterprise plan side by side. For an example of how plan tiers break down, see <NoteLink slug="grok-bot-pricing-plans">Grok Bot Pricing: Which Plans Include the Bot?</NoteLink>. Check four areas in particular:
- **Seats and contract term**: Check how seats are counted, how long the contract runs, and whether seats can be reduced mid-term when employees leave or change roles during an annual contract or can only be reassigned to another person.
- **Usage and credit billing**: Check how much allowance the base subscription includes, how usage is metered, how extra credits are purchased once exceeded, and whether accounts in the same department can share a credit pool.
- **Data and permission controls**: Verify which plan tier includes single sign-on, role-based permissions, and data governance controls. Exact terms always follow the vendor's formal contract.
- **Budgeting and renewal reminders**: Once SaaS pricing moves from monthly billing to annual contracts, include a buffer for variable credit usage in the budget and set a pre-expiration renewal reminder in the procurement ledger.

## AI enterprise plan: FAQ

### If only a few people on our team use the tool, should we buy the AI enterprise plan right away?

During a small trial with a few users and no strict requirements for centralized permissions or data governance, start with individual subscriptions or monthly plans to run daily workflows. Once team adoption grows, long-term high-frequency usage is confirmed, or the company requires centralized account management and contractual data and permission terms, negotiate an annual enterprise contract with the vendor.

### When negotiating a contract, how should buyers choose between annual and monthly billing?

While testing results and comparing tools, choose monthly billing to keep the flexibility to adjust at any time; once the tool is embedded in daily workflows and you need the permission and data controls included in the enterprise plan, sign an annual contract. Before signing an annual deal, clarify how seats are counted and whether seats can be reduced mid-term, following the vendor's formal contract for exact terms.

### How should procurement teams budget when SaaS pricing combines a subscription with credits?

When SaaS pricing pairs a base subscription with extra credit purchases and shifts billing from monthly to annual, both budgeting and renewal reminders need to be updated. Instead of simply multiplying headcount by a fixed monthly rate for the annual budget, budget the fixed base subscription separately from estimated peak-season credit purchases, and configure usage alerts and contract-expiration reminders in the admin console.
