Evaluating influencer marketing ROI by individual tracking links often leads teams to shut down the channel after a small test batch. In an interview uploaded in late January 2026 on the Chinese podcast Kedaibiao Lizheng, Hockey Stick founder Chen Chang said that the most common pattern her team sees is companies buying influencer placements the same way they buy performance marketing. When evaluating influencer marketing results, companies should measure the campaign as a whole, treat single-link clicks as one reference signal, and record multi-month traffic after publication, inquiries or trials, and third-party mentions.

## Why judging each tracking link misleads teams

Chen Chang said she worked on influencer partnerships at Microsoft earlier in her career and later helped Silicon Valley companies with growth and influencer marketing. In the Kedaibiao Lizheng interview, she noted that many repeat founders acquired customers through paid ads in their previous software businesses, where they could track which ad and channel brought each user, how much they spent, and how much they made back. Seeing others invest in influencers, they give each creator a tracking link to check clicks, spend, and conversions; when a small test batch shows zero creators with a positive individual return, they conclude the channel does not work. She said the logic of influencer channels differs from performance advertising.

Influencer posts follow a hit rate. Even strong angles, timing, and creators cannot guarantee every post goes viral. Chen Chang stated in the interview that her team's hit rate is around 10%, and slightly higher in good stretches, meaning 9 out of 10 posts do not take off; calculated alone, the non-hit posts rarely show strong returns. A single breakout post, however, can pull the return of the entire campaign in that content direction into positive territory. She also noted that creator rates rise every quarter, with some creators doubling or tripling their prices in a single quarter; when clients ask to book the exact creators a competitor used six months ago, she tells them prices back then may have been half of today's and the topic was hotter, so copying that list now does not pay off.

## How breakout long tails and multi-touch conversions happen

Creator content carries a long-term brand effect beyond first-day clicks. Chen Chang gave the example in the interview that YouTube videos sometimes continue bringing in traffic three months after publication. Closing the books based only on link clicks from the first few days misses that later traffic.

User conversion often takes several touches. Chen Chang explained in the interview that a user might hear about a product from a friend, watch a long-form YouTube video, and then convert; or see it on a social platform first and then encounter another creator's review while searching, building third-party trust across multiple touches. Before launching a campaign, the team should write down which data categories they will review for influencer marketing evaluation: ongoing traffic over the months after publication, resulting inquiries or trials, and any third-party mentions. ## How to leave co-creation room in creator briefs

Chen Chang summarized several practices in the interview that often cause campaigns to fail: handing creators a plain product sheet, asking for deep hard-sell demos that walk through features one by one, and dictating the exact script. Combined, those practices make campaigns fail because hard-sell posts rarely go viral. She said posts that take off usually start with a story, a timely topic, or a strong opening hook before bringing in the product naturally.

Chen Chang co-creates with creators rather than forcing a rigid script. She shared an example in the interview: a creator proposed making a joke about a recent AWS incident; the client team felt it carried risk because they had been affected too, but they agreed to try it, and the post went viral that same day. She also noted that creators who accept every sponsorship offer may deliver high views, yet their conversion rates are often weak. Company briefs should state what problem the product solves and what claims are off-limits while leaving the delivery to the creator; for balancing creative output with brand sign-off, see <NoteLink slug="ai-video-ads-vs-brand-review">AI Video Ads: Producing More vs. Passing Brand Review</NoteLink>.

## How to set batch budgets and sign long-term deals

Booking only a handful of creators in a test batch gives too small a sample to draw conclusions. The batch size and observation window depend on the company's own budget, and the full batch budget should be accounted for as one overall project at kickoff.

On deal structure, Chen Chang explained in the interview that a first collaboration with a creator is usually flat-fee paid; once the working relationship goes well, teams sign 3-month, 6-month, or 9-month contracts that combine a base fee with a conversion revenue share. She said that structure gives creators an incentive to improve both the posts and the conversions.

## Influencer marketing: FAQ

### If we booked a few creators and did not break even, should we stop right away?

Booking only a few creators gives too small a sample to judge the channel. Chen Chang stated in the interview that her team's hit rate is around 10%, meaning 9 out of 10 posts do not go viral, and the breakout posts pull the return of the full campaign in that content direction into the black. Set the batch size and observation window according to your budget, check first whether you forced feature-heavy scripts or picked creators who take every sponsorship, adjust those practices, and then evaluate results across the whole project.

### If we do not judge success by single links, should we still use tracking links?

Yes. Evaluating success at the project level does not mean stopping link tracking. Record clicks and conversions from tracking links as one signal alongside multi-month traffic after publication, inquiries or trials generated, and third-party mentions.

### Which data categories should an influencer marketing evaluation cover?

Place the unit of evaluation on the entire influencer project. Before kickoff, write down the data categories to review: click and conversion signals from tracking links, ongoing traffic over the months after publication, inquiries or trials generated, and whether third-party mentions appear. Set the exact metrics and targets when the project actually launches rather than pre-setting them in the abstract.
