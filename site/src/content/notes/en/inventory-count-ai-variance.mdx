When AI investigates inventory count variances, the tool only links transaction records, lists candidate causes, and shows anomaly evidence. It does not accept the loss and does not change inventory by default. Once a shortage enters loss handling, the warehouse confirms the physical facts, finance reviews the accounting impact, and the supply chain or finance lead approves anything above the set threshold. Late document entry and misplaced stock are fixed first by entering the missing documents and moving the goods back. Only a verified real loss gets a variance adjustment posted.

## Before investigating, lock which ledger and which point in time to compare

When the count sheet shows overages and shortages, do not let the tool dig through transactions at random. First fix what is being compared and the time cut. Each variance record is tied to a specific item code, batch, warehouse, bin, stock status, unit of measure, and exact count time. Keep a book snapshot just before and just after the count. If goods are received and shipped during the count without a snapshot, the computed difference mixes in business that happened during the count.

Before comparing, also agree on which ledger the physical stock is compared against. The finance general ledger inventory, the warehouse operations inventory, and line-side inventory in the workshop each write back documents on a different schedule. When update times do not line up, false variances appear. Keep the comparison basis strict as well. Accepted goods, goods awaiting inspection, frozen stock, and scrap are each counted separately, and stock in different statuses cannot offset each other. For batch-managed items, check batch by batch. A balanced total does not clear a batch mismatch.

## What the transaction trail can and cannot show

Once the basis is fixed, quantity arithmetic and unit conversion run on deterministic calculation rules. The agent follows the timeline and links the transactions: purchase receipts, sales shipments, transfer-out, in-transit, and transfer-in, production issues and returns, customer and supplier returns, past count adjustments, and voids and reversals. It assembles a readable chain of events. If wave picking quantities are wrong in daily outbound work, that also shows up as a book-to-physical mismatch at count time. For front-end controls on that work, see <NoteLink slug="warehouse-picking-ai-wave-planning">AI Plans Picking Waves: Which Numbers Should Supervisors Watch?</NoteLink>.

Tracing back up the document chain commonly turns up a few kinds of system-side causes. With late document entry, entering the backlog brings the books into line on its own, and no adjustment is needed. With missed steps, such as a return receipt that was not written back or a cross-warehouse transfer confirmed on one side only, the team enters the missing documents one by one from the trail. When bin A is over and bin B is short by the same amount, goods were usually put away or moved to the wrong bin. Another kind is sync mismatches between upstream and downstream business systems.

Keep the tool's candidate causes strictly separate from confirmed causes. For example, a transfer-out record without a completed transfer-in only shows the transfer chain is not closed. It does not mean the goods are lost. Without evidence, the case stays pending, and nobody picks a cause just to close it. Wrong bins, fallen-off labels, moves without scanning, and physical damage cannot be confirmed from back-end records. Warehouse staff go to the floor to recheck, take photos, scan, or recount to supply the evidence.

## How many approval levels shortage handling has, and who accepts the loss

After false variances and missing documents are filtered out, only shortages actually caused by damage, expiry, or theft enter loss handling. The company routes them by amount, quantity, and nature. Small routine variances get fast-track posting, but still need confirmation by both the operator and a reviewer, with details kept. Variances above the company's set threshold, in high-value categories, or with unusual causes go through all five approval steps.

- **Warehouse supervisor first review**: checks the exported variance list and sends back false variances, such as documents not entered in time or transfers still in transit, for document entry.
- **Operations supervisor or warehouse manager review**: for the real variances left after the first review, assigns an owner to recheck on site, and verifies the photo, scan, and recount records.
- **Finance review**: calculates the impact on inventory value and cost, and confirms the adjusted figures reconcile with the general ledger.
- **Supply chain or finance lead confirmation**: for variances above the company's set threshold or of unusual nature, the lead makes the final confirmation.
- **Posting and filing**: after approval, the adjustment is formally posted, and the adjustment documents and recheck evidence are filed together.

## Posting a variance adjustment and preventing repeat issues

When changing book figures, do not overwrite the book quantity with the count quantity. Issue a formal variance adjustment document with the cause category, approvers, and recheck evidence. Inventory permissions can be configured so the agent does not adjust inventory by default, and every accounting change runs only after human approval. If an adjustment posts to the wrong account and needs to be reversed, see <NoteLink slug="agent-rollback-and-accountability">AI Agent Rollback: How to Undo a Bad Action and Who Is Accountable</NoteLink> for rollback and accountability.

To judge whether the process works, do not count how many records were balanced. Track the root-cause confirmation rate, repeat variances, and wrong adjustments. The warehouse and operations teams regularly collect repeat variances of the same kind, and push problems such as missed scans and system sync errors back to the front-end process for fixes.

## Inventory count variance: FAQ

### Accepted stock of an item is short, and the inspection area has exactly the same extra quantity. Can they offset?

No. Accepted, awaiting inspection, frozen, and scrap are different stock statuses, and their totals cannot offset each other. Different batches of the same item cannot be judged by whether the total balances either. Check batch by batch. If the floor check shows staff forgot a status change or entered the wrong batch, enter documents that match the actual business first to set each ledger right.

### Why not let the tool balance inventory automatically after it traces the transactions?

Transaction records only reflect what is already in the system. They cannot confirm whether the goods are physically there. With late document entry, unsynced system interfaces, or misplaced stock, overwriting the book quantity with the count quantity backfires. When the late documents are entered or the misplaced goods turn up, the books show reverse false variances and wrong adjustments, which corrupt inventory value and cost accounting.

### Which approval levels does shortage handling go through?

Routine shortage handling goes through, in order: warehouse supervisor first review to stop false variances, operations supervisor or warehouse manager review with an on-site recheck, and finance review of the impact on inventory value and cost. Variances above the company's set threshold or of unusual nature then go to the supply chain or finance lead for final confirmation. After approval, a variance adjustment document is posted and filed. Small routine variances within the set threshold can use fast-track posting, but still need confirmation and a record from both the operator and a reviewer. Each company sets its own threshold.
