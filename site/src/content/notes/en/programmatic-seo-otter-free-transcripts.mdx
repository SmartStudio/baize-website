Programmatic SEO usually refers to generating pages in bulk from templates and data to capture long-tail searches, whereas Otter turned a proprietary product capability into a free resource others wanted to cite and built backlinks along the way. In an interview uploaded in late January 2026 on the Chinese podcast Kedaibiao Lizheng, former Otter.ai employee Chen Chang explained how Otter ran this play: using its own real-time speech-to-text algorithm, the team provided free written transcripts and live captions for podcasters, video creators, and major tech conferences, sidestepping crowded paid-ad bidding wars among competitors.

## Why bidding on the same keywords yields mediocre results

Many growth teams fall into the same trap: rushing into whatever channel everyone else is using. In the Kedaibiao Lizheng interview, Chen Chang recalled a previous company where many competitors ran paid ads and bid on the exact same keywords, leaving everyone with mediocre performance.

The same crowding repeats in other channels. Chen Chang used influencer marketing as another example: when companies rush into booking creators all at once, creators have more offers than they need, and she stated that creator quotes have been rising every quarter. ## How Otter ran free transcription on its own algorithm

To avoid homogeneous competition, Chen Chang said her team at Otter.ai ran programmatic SEO. She explained that two conditions made it possible at the time: first, Otter owned a proprietary speech recognition algorithm capable of turning speech into text in real time, a capability that was still relatively scarce on the market back then, though she noted everyone can do it now; second, because the underlying algorithm was built in-house, the cost stayed controllable.

With a scarce capability at controllable cost, the team executed two concrete plays:
- **Partnering with content creators**: The team approached podcasters, YouTube creators, and creators on other platforms to transcribe their audio and video into text for free, using the resulting transcripts for Otter's link building and search indexing.
- **Partnering with major industry conferences**: The team also partnered with TechCrunch, Web Summit, and other large conferences. At the venue, large screens beside the stage scrolled real-time captions powered directly by Otter; after the conference ended, many session recordings and transcripts lived on Otter's platform, where viewers could open a page, listen to the audio while reading the text, and click any section to jump straight to that point.

Chen Chang summed up in the interview that this approach amounted to inventing a channel that only their product could run, without crowding into the same keywords or running look-alike ads. She added that she later found company-specific plays at other businesses as well.

## Auditing internal capabilities and calculating free-tier costs

For another company looking to apply this approach, the first step is to audit what it holds internally that others lack and that external creators or industry partners actually need: data, tool capabilities, or industry reference material. Only resources that can be given away for free and that partners want to cite will earn backlinks.

The second step is to calculate the unit cost of free supply. Whatever is offered for free must have a controllable cost. Otter's prerequisite was an in-house algorithm with manageable cost; if the underlying capability requires paying steep per-unit fees to an outside vendor, the wider the free offering scales, the heavier the budget strain becomes.

## Managing content authorization and page usefulness

When turning partner content into search pages, both authorization and reader value require strict oversight. Link building through partner content requires permission from the content owner, with usage terms and attribution agreed before transcribing or republishing someone else's material.

At the same time, the resulting pages must serve the reader; piling up thin pages serves no purpose. Turning long audio into timestamped transcripts that let readers search and verify quotes saves visitors time when they land on the page. ## Programmatic SEO: FAQ

### Can a team apply this approach without a proprietary algorithm?

Yes, by swapping the underlying asset while keeping the exchange logic. Otter used a proprietary speech algorithm that was scarce and cost-controllable at the time; a company without a proprietary algorithm can check whether it holds exclusive industry statistics, curated compliance checklists, or lightweight calculators. As long as the marginal cost is low and the resource helps the target audience, the team can trade it for collaborations with industry media, creators, or conferences.

### Will programmatic SEO pages generated in bulk be treated as spam?

That depends on whether the page helps the reader. Filling empty templates just to pad word count and page volume does not help readers and accomplishes nothing; by contrast, full conference transcripts or timestamped in-depth interview texts carry high information density and help readers locate exact quotes quickly, giving those pages a real chance to capture long-tail search traffic.

### What should teams watch out for when using free tools or content for link building?

Focus on three rules: first, calculate the marginal cost so offering the free service does not strain the budget; second, secure formal permission from content owners in advance and clarify usage, placement, and attribution before transcribing or organizing their audio or video; third, keep the landing page readable so visitors can finish what they came to read and see what your product does along the way.
