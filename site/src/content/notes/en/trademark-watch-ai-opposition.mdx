A similar mark reported by a trademark watch tool is only a lead to investigate. The similarity score from the algorithm is not a legal judgment. Whether to oppose a mark in a preliminary approval announcement is decided by legal or the IP lead together with an outside trademark agent. They check, item by item, whether the goods or services are identical or similar, whether the company has standing as a prior right holder or interested party, whether the evidence on hand is complete, how many days remain before the deadline, and how much the mark would affect the actual business. Marketing helps collect evidence of use, and management decides whether to spend the fee.

## What to check first when the watch reports a similar mark

After each issue of the preliminary approval announcement is published, some tools can run a trademark watch pass. They screen out announced marks that look similar and give each a similarity score. When legal or the IP lead receives the results, they first remove obvious false positives. They log the marks to follow up in a watch sheet, with the announcement date, the deadline and the owner. They do not treat the tool's similarity score as a legal judgment.

The search and comparison step can be set to run automatically on a schedule. At the step of filing an opposition with the authority, the workflow needs a boundary where it stops and waits for a human decision. For where an automated watch agent should stop and hand over to a person, see <NoteLink slug="agent-stop-conditions-decision-log">Agent Safety Guardrails: When to Stop an Unattended Run</NoteLink>. Under the official filing guide, one opposition application can target only one preliminarily approved mark. If the same party has several similar marks in one announcement, the watch sheet splits them into separate entries, each followed up and documented on its own.

## Which judgments decide whether to oppose

For each similar mark logged in the watch sheet, legal or the outside trademark agent checks goods similarity, standing, days left and business impact, and then gives a recommendation on whether to file a formal opposition. Management then approves whether to spend the fee.

- **Whether the goods or services are identical or similar**: looking at how similar the marks look is not enough. Check whether the goods or services designated in the other party's announcement are identical or similar to the company's business.
- **Whether the grounds match the company's standing**: Article 33 of the current Trademark Law provides that an opposition to a preliminarily approved mark can be filed within three months from the date of announcement. Anyone can oppose on absolute grounds. Only a prior right holder or interested party can oppose on relative grounds such as prior rights. A company without standing cannot file on relative grounds.
- **How many days remain before the deadline**: compare the deadline in the watch sheet with today, and assess whether there is enough time to gather evidence internally, have the agent draft the filing, and finish internal approval.
- **Business impact and fee approval**: use marketing's input on how much the other party's registration would affect the company's business, and ask management to decide whether to spend the fee.

When legal or the trademark agent drafts the grounds for opposition and has used a search tool to find provisions and cases, they check every citation against the original text before filing. For how to verify search results before citing them, see <NoteLink slug="legal-research-ai-case-check">Cases From a Legal Research AI: How to Verify Them Before You Cite</NoteLink>.

## How to prepare proof of standing and evidence of prior use

Once management approves the fee and the company decides to oppose on relative grounds such as prior rights, legal and marketing need to watch the requirements for the first filing. Under the official filing guide, a first opposition on relative grounds must include proof of standing as a prior right holder or interested party at the same time. The application form cannot go in first with the proof of standing left out.

The documents depend on the basis of the right. An opposition based on a prior registered or applied-for mark lists the registration or application number. An opposition based on prior use needs evidence from marketing, working with legal, that shows the mark, the goods or services, the date of use and the user. If proof of standing is ready at the first filing but more evidence of use is still being collected, the applicant can tick the option for supplementary evidence on the application form and submit it within three months from the filing date.

## How to track the opposition deadline, and the transition to the new law

The opposition deadline in the watch sheet must be calculated exactly under the official rules. One day late and the opposition can no longer be filed. Article 33 of the current Trademark Law sets the opposition period at three months. According to the official filing guide, the period starts on the day after the preliminary approval announcement. If the last day falls on a public holiday, it moves to the first working day after the holiday. Legal records the announcement date, the deadline and the owner in the watch sheet, and triggers reminders a number of days in advance that the company sets.

The shorter period under the amended law also needs preparation now. The amended Trademark Law, adopted on June 26, 2026, takes effect on January 1, 2027. It shortens the opposition period from three months to two, which leaves marketing less time to gather evidence and management less time to approve fees. The China National Intellectual Property Administration released draft transitional measures for comment on September 28, 2026. The draft splits cases by preliminary approval announcement date: marks announced before January 1, 2027 get three months, and marks announced after get two. The final measures govern. A company's trademark watch sheet can already set reminders against the shorter two-month period.

## Trademark watch: FAQ

### The other party has several similar marks in the same announcement. Can we file one combined opposition?

No. Under the official filing guide, one opposition application can target only one preliminarily approved mark. If the trademark watch finds several similar marks from the same party at once, legal assesses the business impact of each, asks management to approve the fee for each, and prepares and files a separate application and evidence for each one.

### Marketing cannot gather all the evidence of prior use right away. Can we file the opposition first?

Proof of standing and the basic documents must be filed first. More detailed evidence can follow. For a first opposition on relative grounds, the proof of standing as a prior right holder or interested party must be filed with the application. If more evidence of use is still needed, tick the supplementary evidence option on the application form at filing, and submit it within three months from the filing date.

### What can we do if we missed the three-month opposition deadline?

Once the statutory opposition period ends, no opposition can be filed against that preliminarily approved mark. Whether other legal procedures remain available needs a separate assessment by a trademark agent, which this note does not cover. Day to day, the safeguard is recording the announcement date and the deadline in the watch sheet and warning early. After the amended Trademark Law takes effect on January 1, 2027, the opposition period shrinks to two months, so internal handling has to move even earlier.
