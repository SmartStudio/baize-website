A PLG model rarely works as the direct sales motion for high-ticket enterprise AI products with complex setup. PLG stands for product-led growth, where users sign up, onboard, and pay on their own. In an interview uploaded in late January 2026 on the Chinese podcast Kedaibiao Lizheng, Hockey Stick founder Chen Chang and the host discussed product-channel fit. According to Chen Chang, high-ticket enterprise software relies on founder-led sales early on and a dedicated sales team later; testing self-serve growth just to avoid hiring salespeople, while nobody on the team speaks directly with the market, usually produces negative online test feedback.

## How high-ticket software finds customers

In the Kedaibiao Lizheng interview, Chen Chang explained product-channel fit: after finding a market, a team still has to find acquisition channels that match both the product and the team. She stated that her team has run more than 20 channels and keeps a table mapping channels to product types. For enterprise products, she starts with average deal size. By the threshold she gave in the interview, products with an average deal size above $100,000 rely on founder-led sales in the early stage and a sales team later on.

For products in that bracket, Chen Chang listed several motions: inbound uses public relations, offline ads, events, and conference sponsorships; outbound relies on sales development representatives finding leads, cleaning lists, sending emails, or making calls, and for where to keep human sign-off on outbound emails see <NoteLink slug="crm-outbound-agent-send-approval">Which CRM Outbound Agent Emails Need Human Approval Before Sending?</NoteLink>; partnerships with upstream or downstream companies that share similar target customers also work well. Selling to large enterprises also requires account-based marketing, because an enterprise group includes the decision-maker alongside procurement, finance, IT, and other departments, and each role needs different materials.

## Why copying self-serve growth blindly causes trouble

Chen Chang said in the interview that pure PLG fits products that users can pick up immediately, cost relatively little, and can be bought with a credit card; channels like influencers, Reddit, and Product Hunt fit prosumer or consumer-leaning products better. The host of Kedaibiao Lizheng shared his own experience during the interview: he spent two years at Statsig, an enterprise-sales software company whose founders had limited marketing and sales experience and hired people later to build the sales team. The host said they tried influencer marketing, Reddit, search engine optimization to some extent, and Product Hunt, and found none of them worked well because what they were selling was too expensive. He added that Chen Chang's framework ruled out many wrong options that had all happened at Statsig.

Chen Chang advised teams in the interview against choosing PLG just to avoid hiring sales reps. She has met teams with products clearly suited for sales: technical products with hard setup, or products priced at 10,000 to 20,000, where a team based in Europe or China did not want to hire salespeople in the United States and tried self-serve growth first. She said that path is difficult, especially when no founder goes to learn the market firsthand and the team only spends money testing a few online channels, which most likely returns negative feedback.

## When developer tools can make self-serve trials work

Can technical products run self-serve growth? Chen Chang said in the interview that many developer-facing products are technical and still run PLG, provided the entry barrier stays relatively low: a few developers can connect an API or install an SDK, get it running, and see the product's benefit quickly.

She pointed to Twilio as an example: Twilio's enterprise contracts can be very large, yet its self-serve side let developers connect an API and use it right away with strong documentation and frequent hackathons in the developer community, before sales reps stepped in later to expand contract value. Applied to a company's own product, a high-ticket product can keep a self-serve trial entry so technical staff experience the results first, while closing the deal stays with sales.

## Three questions to ask before picking a channel

Before committing to a primary acquisition channel, the team should answer three questions: how much a single deal costs, whether users can onboard without human help, and whether the person paying is the person using the product. For working through who pays, see <NoteLink slug="ai-monetization-dropbox-lesson">Is OpenAI Retracing Dropbox's Path? AI Monetization Starts With Who Pays</NoteLink>.

When the price is high, setup is hard, and the buyer is separate from the user, start with founder-led sales and partner channels. Test one acquisition channel seriously at a time, and write down before testing how long the test will run, which metric to watch, and who owns it before deciding the next step from the results.

## PLG model: FAQ

### At what deal size should a team stop relying on pure self-serve growth?

The threshold Chen Chang gave in the interview is an average deal size above $100,000, where early sales come from the founder and later sales come from a dedicated team; she also noted that products priced at 10,000 to 20,000 with complex setup face a tough time when teams try self-serve growth just to avoid hiring sales reps. The $100,000 figure is Chen Chang's benchmark from the interview, and companies should judge by their own setup complexity and customer buying process.

### Can developer-facing technical tools use a PLG model?

Yes, provided the entry barrier is relatively low. As Chen Chang explained in the interview, if a few developers can connect an API or install an SDK, get it running, and see the benefit quickly, supported by clear documentation and developer community hackathons, the product can run self-serve growth early on and rely on sales later to grow enterprise deal sizes.

### How should high-ticket products select and test acquisition channels?

Before selecting a channel, check how much a deal costs, whether users can onboard without help, and whether the buyer is the user. When prices are high, setup is complex, and buyers are separate from users, start with founder-led sales, partner collaborations, SDR outbound, and conference sponsorships. Test one channel seriously at a time, and write down the test duration, target metric, and owner before launching.
