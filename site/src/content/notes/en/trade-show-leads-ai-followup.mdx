For trade show leads, AI handles business card entry, field cleaning, and first-pass grading suggestions. Who follows up with each customer follows rules the sales lead set before the show. The AI does not decide ownership. Existing customers, and contacts that already have an owner in the CRM, go back to that owner. When two booth reps both claim a lead, the sales manager checks the booth records and decides. Assigned leads get a first-contact deadline by grade. Leads not followed up in time are taken back and reassigned. The person who confirmed the entry corrects any misread fields.

## How far AI business card entry goes

Paper business cards collected at a show are many and mixed. Booth reps or sales assistants can start card entry by photographing cards with the tool, or by uploading images in bulk after the booth closes. Some tools can read printed text in several foreign languages and handwritten notes, extract name, company, title, phone, email, website, and address, and fill the matching lead fields. The tool only reads what is printed on the card. Before saving, the person entering it checks each field and adds the business details that only the conversation reveals: products of interest, show name, and lead grade.

To make show ROI easier to calculate afterward, new leads can be configured in the back end to carry the show name and a source tag by default. For how to record first-touch source across multichannel inquiries and offline contact, see <NoteLink slug="export-inquiry-whatsapp-email-attribution">How to Record Lead Source When AI Sorts WhatsApp and Email Inquiries</NoteLink>. Before entering the database, leads also go through cleaning: validate email and phone formats, fill in missing company information, and filter out duplicates, merging scattered records of the same visitor into one clean profile.

## Write the assignment rules before the show

Before the show opens, the sales lead works with marketing and sales assistants to settle two things. The first is what counts as a valid lead. Agree on the required fields first: customer type (end customer, distributor, partner), industry, product interest, budget, purchase timing, decision role, and the source show or booth. The second is which rules decide assignment after leads enter the database.

Export sales teams commonly assign leads in three ways. Split by rules on country or region, customer industry, product line, and customer grade. Put all leads from the show into one shared pool and assign them in rotation to the reps who attended. Or let whoever scanned the card at the booth own the follow-up. The sales lead picks one based on how the team divides work and configures it in the CRM before the show. The AI only puts the cleaned list, tagged with first-pass grade suggestions, into the set routing channel. It does not change the assignment logic.

## How to decide duplicate claims and existing customers

Common trade show lead problems are multiple sign-ups and duplicate claims. One overseas buyer may scan the code on a booth display, then hand a business card to a rep, then fill in the inquiry form on the website back at the hotel. Or two reps at the same booth may both talk to the same person and each enter the card. Some tools can mark these records as duplicates and hold them before formal assignment.

Resolve duplicates and ownership conflicts in this order: existing before new, and only on recorded evidence. Once the contact is confirmed as an existing customer with past deals, or the CRM already has an active owner, the new record merges into the existing profile and stays with that owner. The colleague who met them at the booth gets an assist record. If the customer is new and two booth reps both claim it, the process can be configured to put the lead on hold as unassigned. The sales manager reviews the upload time of each card, the handwritten notes, and the booth conversation records, decides, and then locks the owner.

## First-contact deadlines and taking back overdue leads

Assigning a lead is only the start. The back end can be configured to notify the owner at assignment and create a first-contact task. For high-intent leads, it can also notify the rep's manager or hand the lead to a senior rep. If a tool helps draft the first post-show outreach or follow-up email, see <NoteLink slug="crm-outbound-agent-send-approval">Which CRM Outbound Agent Emails Need Human Approval Before Sending?</NoteLink> for the checks before sending.

Customers at different intent levels get different first-contact deadlines. One practice is to contact high-intent customers within one day and follow up with medium-intent ones within two days. Set the countdown in the task using the number of days the company chooses. When a deadline is close and no follow-up is logged, the system can remind the owner and the manager. When the deadline passes with no contact, the sales manager takes the lead back and reassigns it to another rep. Visitors with no clear purchase plan or no current need do not belong on a front-line rep's short-term chase list. Mark them for long-term follow-up and put them on a nurture list for the marketing team's regular outreach.

## Trade show leads: FAQ

### Is it fine to enter all the cards after we get back from the show?

If entry waits until after the show, booth reps forget details about the products and needs each visitor discussed, and the contact window for high-intent customers slips. A safer practice is for the booth rep or a traveling sales assistant to photograph and confirm cards each day after the hall closes. While memory is fresh, they add products of interest, budget, and purchase timing to the record, so first contact goes out to high-intent leads early, within the number of days the company sets.

### Should booth visitors with no near-term purchase plan go into the sales follow-up pool?

Create their records as usual, but route them differently. Under the grading standard set before the show, visitors without a near-term purchase plan or a clear current need are marked for long-term follow-up and put on the nurture list after entry. Marketing reaches them with routine new product material or post-show follow-ups. This keeps the source tag and the customer record, without taking front-line reps away from high-intent inquiries.

### When AI enters business cards and misreads something, who corrects it?

Whoever uploads and confirms the card at the booth is first responsible for accuracy. After extraction, the booth rep or sales assistant checks each field before saving. For cards with handwritten notes, less common foreign languages, glare, or blurry print, they check phone numbers and email spelling closely. All manual edits stay in the lead record for reference. If a later email bounces or a call shows the number is wrong, the current owner corrects it in the customer profile.
